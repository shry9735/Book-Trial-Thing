=== A4-ARRIVE ===

The van turns off the highway onto a county road, then onto a smaller one, then onto a gravel one. Fields on both sides, brown and stubbly after the harvest. A barn. A silo. A dog that runs along the fence beside you, barking happily, until the fence ends.

Bitsy has the last packet's position pinned on the map. D.U.D.E.A.D. has the same spot circled on his paper map in pencil.

"Getting close," he says.

=== A4-WRONGSIDE ===

D.U.D.E.A.D. frowns at his paper map. Then at Bitsy's laptop. Then at the paper map again.

"Kid," he says. "Where are we going?"

"Northeast," Bitsy says. "The prediction. The landing ellipse."

"Friday's prediction," D.U.D.E.A.D. says. "Skylark didn't fly Friday's winds. It flew today's."

He's right. All morning, you've been driving toward the oval on the printed map, the one from last night. Skylark followed the six o'clock forecast, the one nobody ran. Its real landing spot is fifteen kilometers east, across the county, on the other side of a river with only two bridges.

Mr. Torres doesn't say anything. He just turns the van around and starts looking for the nearest bridge.

=== A4-GASDETOUR ===

There's a soft *ding* from the dashboard. A little orange gas-pump light comes on.

Mr. Torres looks at the needle. It's resting on the E.

"Fumes," he says. "We're on fumes."

Spark checks his phone for gas stations. The nearest one is in a town twenty-five kilometers away, in the wrong direction. There's nothing closer. Out here, there are more cows than gas pumps.

You think about the station at the on-ramp, with three cars waiting.

"Can we make it there and back?" Bitsy asks.

"To there, yes," Mr. Torres says. "Back here, only if we fill up. Which we will." He turns the van around.

=== A4-SPLASH ===

You hear the reservoir before you see it: water slapping against rocks. Then the road curves, and there it is, Clearwater Reservoir, wide and gray and choppy, stretching off into the distance.

The last packet came from the middle of it.

Mr. Torres parks by the boat ramp. You all run to the edge and stare out at the water. Bitsy has the binoculars.

"There," she says, pointing. "There!"

About forty meters out, bobbing on the little waves, is Skylark. Foam floats. The box is upright, the parachute spread out on the water beside it like a red lily pad. It's drifting slowly away from the shore.

"Forty meters," Spark says. "That's nothing. It's like half a swimming pool. I could wade out."

=== A4-SPLASH-NO ===

You take one step toward the water. Mr. Torres's hand is on your shoulder before your shoe touches it.

"No," he says. Not loud. Just completely certain.

"It's only forty meters," Spark says.

"It's October," Mr. Torres says. "That water is about eight degrees. Cold water takes your breath away, and it saps your strength faster than you'd believe. The bottom is mud, and nobody here knows how deep it gets or where it drops off." He doesn't take his hand off your shoulder. "A box is a box. We can build another box."

D.U.D.E.A.D. nods slowly. "Nobody goes in, kid. Nobody. We call someone with a boat."

Spark sits down on a rock by the shore. "Okay," he says. "Okay. Who do we call?"

=== A4-SPLASH-RANGER ===

There's a sign by the boat ramp with the park office's phone number on it. The ranger who answers is named Officer Reyes. She listens to the whole story, asks two very sensible questions, and then laughs.

"I've pulled a lot of things out of that reservoir," she says. "Never a weather balloon. I'll have to wait till morning, though. The boat's out for repairs."

So you go home without Skylark.

Overnight, the wind pushes the little box across the reservoir. Water seeps in through the camera hole, the vent and the seams. By morning it's half full.

At nine o'clock Sunday morning, Officer Reyes meets you at the boat ramp and hands you Skylark, dripping, with a rubber glove.

=== A4-TREE ===

The forest edge is a wall of tall pine trees, dark green and swaying. The last packet led you right to it. And there, about twelve meters up in the tallest pine, is Skylark.

It's hanging by its parachute, which is wrapped twice around a branch. The box swings gently in the wind, turning slowly. The camera lens catches the sun.

"Twelve meters," Spark says, squinting. "That's like a four-story building."

"Nobody climbs," Mr. Torres says immediately. He says it before anyone's even thought about it. "Not a chance. Not that tree, not any tree. Ideas?"

Everyone looks up. Spark finds a fishing reel in the van's emergency kit. Across the road, there's a house with a big garage and a tractor in the yard. And the wind is picking up; the box swings a little farther each time.

> **ENGINEERING NOTE — Levers and Pulleys**
> A branch is a lever: the farther from the trunk you pull on it, the more it bends for the same force. A line thrown over a branch acts like a simple pulley. It changes the direction of your pull, so you can tug *down* from the ground and the branch moves *down* too. With a long enough pole or a line in the right place, a person on the ground can do what would otherwise mean climbing.

=== A4-TREE-LINE ===

"Fishing line," you say.

Spark ties a lead fishing weight to the end of the line, and you take turns trying to throw it over the branch. It's much harder than it looks. The weight goes too low. It goes too far left. It hits the branch and bounces back. It wraps around a completely different branch and has to be yanked loose.

Twenty throws. Twenty-five. Your arm aches.

On the thirty-first try, Bitsy throws it, and the weight sails up and over the branch right next to the parachute and drops down the other side.

Everyone cheers. You tie the line to both ends and pull, slowly and evenly. The branch bends, then bends more, until the chute slips off the end. Skylark drops into the pine needles with a soft *pumf*.

=== A4-TREE-POLE ===

"Let's ask the neighbors," you say.

The house across the road belongs to Mr. Okafor, who answers the door in a sweater with a cat on his shoulder. He listens to the whole story without interrupting.

"A balloon," he says. "To space. And you kids built the box." He shakes his head. "In my day we got excited about a paper airplane." Then he goes into his garage and comes out with a pole saw, a long, telescoping pole with a hook and a saw blade on the end.

It reaches nine meters. With the van parked underneath, and Mr. Torres standing on the van's roof rack holding the pole, it reaches just far enough. The hook catches the parachute, and one gentle tug pulls it free.

Skylark drops into the pine needles. Mr. Okafor nods approvingly. "Now tell me everything," he says. "From the beginning."

=== A4-TREE-WAIT ===

"Wait," you say. "The wind's picking up. It'll blow it down."

So you wait. You sit on the van's back bumper and watch. Every gust makes the box swing, and every swing, everyone holds their breath. The parachute flutters and twists.

It doesn't come down.

An hour passes, then two. The wind blows harder, and the parachute wraps even tighter around the branch with every gust. If anything, the box is more stuck now than when you arrived.

"It's not going to fall," Spark says finally. "The wind's tying it on."

=== A4-FENCE ===

The last packet puts Skylark in a harvested cornfield behind a white farmhouse. You can see the field from the road: rows of short, broken cornstalks, brown dirt, a long way across.

There's a wire fence along the road. On the gate, there's a sign: **PRIVATE PROPERTY — NO TRESPASSING.**

Mr. Torres has pulled over and is on the phone with Ms. Chen, giving her an update with his back turned.

Spark is already looking at the fence. It's only waist high. "We could be over, in, and out in five minutes," he says. "Nobody would even notice."

The farmhouse has a porch and a light on in the kitchen. Somebody's home.

=== A4-HOPPED ===

"Let's go," you say.

You and Spark climb over the fence. It's easy. You're halfway across the field, stepping between the stubble rows, when the screen door of the farmhouse bangs open.

"*Hey!*"

A woman in a barn jacket is walking toward you fast. Mr. Torres has dropped his phone call and is jogging toward her from the road.

Her name is Mrs. Lindqvist. She owns this field and this fence and that sign. She explains, not loudly but very clearly, that people crossing her fences have left gates open, scared her cattle, and broken things. She doesn't know you. She doesn't know about your balloon. All she knows is that two strangers climbed her fence while she watched.

Mr. Torres apologizes for a long time. You apologize too. She listens, but her arms stay crossed.

"Come back tomorrow," she says finally. "With your teacher. Ask first. Then we'll see. Right now, I want you off my land."

=== A4-KNOCK ===

"Let's ask," you say.

You walk up the gravel driveway and knock on the farmhouse door. A woman in a barn jacket answers, with a cup of coffee in her hand and a very surprised look on her face.

Her name is Mrs. Lindqvist. You explain everything: the balloon, the edge of space, the box, the radio, the field. Bitsy shows her the map on the laptop, with the little red pin in the middle of her field.

Mrs. Lindqvist listens to the whole thing without interrupting. Then she puts down her coffee and picks up her truck keys.

"My grandson would love this," she says. "He's always taking things apart. Hop in the back of the truck. It's a big field, and I know where the soft spots are."

Two minutes later you're bouncing across the stubble in the bed of a pickup truck, holding on to the sides, with D.U.D.E.A.D. sitting on the tailgate like he owns it.

=== A4-FIND ===

Mrs. Lindqvist stops the truck in the middle of the field and turns off the engine. Everyone climbs out.

Brown stubble in every direction. Wind. Crows. No foam box anywhere in sight.

Bitsy looks at the map, then around at the field, then back at the map. "It should be around here," she says. "Somewhere."

How big "somewhere" is depends on where that last packet came from.

=== A4-SQUINT ===

Everyone spreads out and scans the field. It should be easy. You're looking for a box, something square in a field full of crooked stalks.

But the plowed dirt is dark brown, almost black where it's damp, and the afternoon sun is low now, throwing long shadows from every row. And you're looking for a box covered in black galaxy.

"There!" Spark shouts, pointing. Everyone runs. It's a rock.

"There!" It's a shadow. "There!" It's an old tire.

Bitsy shades her eyes and turns slowly in a full circle. "I made it look like space," she says. "Space is really hard to see in a dark field."

D.U.D.E.A.D. scans the field beside her. "Orange would be screaming at us right now," he says. Not mean. Just true.

> **ENGINEERING NOTE — Contrast**
> We spot things because they're different from what's around them: different color, different brightness, different shape. That difference is called contrast. Safety orange is used on road cones and hunting vests because almost nothing in nature is that color, so it stands out against green, brown, gray and white. A black box in dark dirt has almost no contrast, especially in low light. Design for where it will be found, not just where it'll be seen.

=== A4-WIDE ===

The last packet came from high up, and the wind pushed Skylark sideways for minutes after that. Bitsy's circle on the map covers about two square kilometers. That's four or five big fields, a creek and a patch of woods.

Skylark is still sending packets, though. It's sitting on the ground somewhere, sending, sending, sending. You just can't hear it from far away.

That's what Spark's been building.

He pulls it out of the van: three pieces of steel tape measure, bolted at right angles to a length of PVC pipe, with a cable running to a handheld radio. It looks like a homemade TV antenna.

"Directional antenna," he says proudly. "It only hears in one direction: where it's pointing. Point it at Skylark and it gets loud. Point it away and it goes quiet."

> **ENGINEERING NOTE — Triangulation**
> One direction tells you a line, not a place: Skylark is somewhere along it. But take a bearing from a second spot and you get a second line, and where the two lines cross is where Skylark is. A third line makes a tiny triangle and checks your work. Sailors, hikers and radio hunters have found things this way for centuries. It's geometry doing the walking for you.

=== A4-TRI ===

You climb to the top of a little rise at the edge of the field. Spark sweeps the antenna slowly in a circle. The radio crackles quietly, then louder, then quiet again. He sweeps back to the loudest spot and holds it there.

"*That* way," he says. Bitsy takes a compass reading and draws a straight pencil line on the paper map.

Then you walk to the corner of the next field and do it again: sweep, listen, loudest, compass, line. Then a third time, from the creek bridge.

Three pencil lines on the map. They don't meet in one exact point, but they make a tiny triangle about the size of your thumbnail, in the middle of a field.

"There," Bitsy says, putting her finger on it. "It's in there."

=== A4-WANDER ===

"Just follow the loudest," you say.

Spark points the antenna, finds the loudest direction, and you all start walking that way. After a few minutes he checks again, and it's louder now, but in a slightly different direction. You change course.

Then it gets quieter. Then louder. The signal bounces off barns and trees and the side of a hill, and every few minutes it seems to come from somewhere new.

You zigzag across one field, a creek, another field and a muddy ditch. Your sneakers are soaked and caked with mud. Spark's arm is shaking from holding up the antenna.

After an hour and a half, you're finally in the right field, and the signal is strong.

"Should've just drawn lines," Bitsy mutters.

=== A4-NEAR ===

You're close now. Very close. Somewhere within a couple of hundred meters.

Spark holds up the antenna, sweeps it around... and frowns. He sweeps it again.

"It's loud *everywhere*," he says. "Every direction. It's maxed out."

He turns in a full circle. The radio crackles at full volume whether he points it north, south, east or west, or straight down at his own sneakers.

"Is the antenna broken?" Bitsy asks.

"No," Spark says. "I think it's too close. The signal's so strong it's coming in from everywhere, even the back."

> **ENGINEERING NOTE — Receiver Overload**
> A receiver is built to pick up very faint signals. When the transmitter is close, the signal is so strong that it floods in from every direction, even through the "deaf" sides of a directional antenna. It's like trying to tell which direction a shout is coming from while someone shouts right into your ear. Radio hunters fix it by making the signal weaker on purpose: turning down the gain, or using their own body as a shield.

=== A4-CIRCLES ===

"Keep going," you say. "It'll sort itself out."

Spark keeps sweeping. You walk ten steps in one direction, and it's still loud everywhere. Ten steps in another. Loud. You walk in a circle, then a bigger circle, then a figure eight.

It doesn't sort itself out. The closer you get, the worse it gets. The radio just roars, all the time, in every direction.

Bitsy finally pulls up the antenna's manual on her phone. "*When close to the transmitter,*" she reads, "*hold the antenna against your body and turn until the signal is weakest. You will be facing away from the transmitter.*"

Spark lowers the antenna. "We've been walking in circles for an hour."

"Yep," says D.U.D.E.A.D.

=== A4-CLOSE ===

You're close enough now that Skylark must be somewhere within a few dozen meters. The radio is no help this close; it's loud everywhere.

Now it's down to the beeper.

Everyone stops walking. Mrs. Lindqvist turns off her truck's engine. Spark sets down the antenna. Even the crows seem to go quiet.

"Listen," D.U.D.E.A.D. says.

=== A4-BEEP ===

At first you hear nothing but wind. Then, faintly, so faintly you aren't sure it's real:

*beep... beep... beep...*

High and thin, like a smoke alarm far away in another house. Somewhere in the stubble.

"I hear it!" Bitsy whispers. "Which way?"

Nobody can tell. The sound seems to come from everywhere, carried by the wind. It's the hardest kind of sound to find: high-pitched, short and repeating.

D.U.D.E.A.D. tilts his head to one side. The two round yellow pieces on the sides of his head, the ones that look like headphones, are microphones. Good ones.

"Got it," he says. "I hear it fine. Want me to walk it down?"

=== A4-DUDE ===

"Go for it," you say.

D.U.D.E.A.D. sets off across the field in his big yellow sneakers. He walks a few steps, stops, tilts his head, turns a little and walks again. Left. Straight. A little right. Everyone follows behind him in a line, trying not to talk.

Row 28. Row 29. Row 30.

He stops in row 31 and points down at the dirt between two broken cornstalks.

There it is. Skylark, sitting upright in the stubble, beeping away, its parachute draped across the next row like a red blanket.

"Here," D.U.D.E.A.D. says. "You're welcome."

=== A4-LINE ===

"Spread out," you say. "Walk the rows."

Everyone lines up at the edge of the field, three rows apart. Mrs. Lindqvist joins in too. Then you all walk forward slowly, side by side, scanning the ground and listening. The beeping gets louder, then softer, then louder, depending on the wind.

It takes forty minutes. Your neck hurts from looking down.

Then Bitsy stops dead.

"Found it," she says. "With my foot."

She's standing right next to Skylark. She nearly stepped on it. It's sitting upright in the stubble, beeping away, its parachute draped across the next row.

=== A4-SILENTGRID ===

Silence. No beeping. Not even faintly.

Why there's no beep depends on what's in your Mission Log:

If you ticked **NO BEEPER**, there's nothing in the box that could beep. You took it out at the weigh-in.

If you ticked **HARD LANDING**, Skylark hit the ground so hard that the battery clip popped loose. No power means no beeper, and no radio either.

If you ticked **BEEPER LISTENS TO PRESSURE ONLY** and **PRESSURE SENSOR BAGGED**, the beeper is waiting for the pressure sensor to say *below 2 kilometers*. But the sensor in its bag is still reading the air it was sealed with, so it may never say it.

Either way, you're looking for a foam box in a big field with your eyes alone. The sun is getting lower.

=== A4-GRID ===

"Grid search," you say. "No skipping."

Mrs. Lindqvist has a roll of bright pink flagging tape in her truck. You tie a strip to a cornstalk at the end of every tenth row. Then you walk the rows in order, everyone side by side, one row each, eyes on the ground, all the way to the end of the field. Then you shift over and come back.

It's slow. It's boring. Your neck aches and your feet are cold.

Row 40. Row 44. Row 47.

"Here!" Spark calls.

It's sitting in the dirt between two rows, the parachute crumpled beside it, silent.

=== A4-RANDOM ===

"Split up," you say. "Cover more ground."

Everyone heads off in a different direction. Spark goes north. Bitsy goes toward the creek. You head for the far corner.

An hour later you all meet back at the truck. Nobody's found it. Comparing notes, you realize that Spark and Bitsy both searched the same strip near the creek, and nobody searched the middle of the field at all. You've walked a long way, mostly over the same ground twice.

You try again, this time with someone calling out directions. It's better, but the light is going.

=== A4-RECOVER ===

There it is.

From a distance, it's just a shape on the ground. Then it's a box. Then it's Skylark: real, scuffed, muddy, *found*.

Everyone runs. Spark gets there first and drops to his knees beside it. Bitsy is right behind him. Even Mr. Torres is jogging.

Skylark went higher than any airplane, higher than almost every cloud, came all the way back down, and it's sitting right here.

=== A4-WRECK ===

But it doesn't look good.

Skylark hit the ground at seventy kilometers an hour, and the foam box shows it: one corner caved in, a crack running down the side, and the lid half torn off. The parachute is still trapped in Bitsy's sleeve, wrapped in strings that twisted around it during the fall.

Inside, the battery clip has popped loose from the batteries. Everything is dark: no lights, no radio, no beeper. It's been that way since the moment it landed. The camera board is snapped in half.

Bitsy gently lifts the box, holding it together with both hands.

"The cards," she says. "Maybe the cards are okay. They're small. They're tough."

Nobody can send an END command to a box with no power. Whatever was on the cards when it hit the ground is all that's on them now.

=== A4-SHUTOFF ===

Skylark is scuffed and muddy, with a little scrape across one side. But its lights are still blinking. The radio is still sending. If the beeper's working, it's still beeping right in your ear, very loud now.

Spark reaches into the box for the battery clip. "Let's save the battery," he says. "Pull the power. It's done its job."

Bitsy's hand shoots out and grabs his wrist.

"Wait," she says. "The log file."

> **ENGINEERING NOTE — Safe Shutdown**
> Computers keep some data in temporary memory before it's saved for good. A proper shutdown tells the computer to finish saving everything first, then turn off. Pulling the power skips that step, and anything not yet saved is gone. That's why your computer warns you not to unplug a drive without ejecting it. On a long mission, the shutdown is part of the mission.

=== A4-YANK ===

"It's fine," you say. "Pull it."

Spark pulls the battery clip. *Click.* The lights go dark. The beeper stops. The radio stops. Skylark is just a box again.

Bitsy lets out a long breath. "Okay," she says. "Okay. Let's look at it at home."

Spark picks up Skylark, carrying it in both hands like Mr. Torres did yesterday, and starts walking back toward the road. Bitsy walks beside him. The sticky note that says **END!!!** is on her laptop, in her backpack, in the van.

=== A4-SHUTDOWN ===

"Send END," you say.

Bitsy opens her laptop right there in the dirt, types E-N-D and hits enter. The command goes out through the antenna in Spark's hands.

On the box, the status light blinks twice, quickly: *file closed.* Bitsy waits three more seconds, just to be safe. Then she nods.

"Now," she says.

Spark flips the switch. The lights go dark. The beeper stops. Skylark sits there in the stubble, quiet for the first time in seven hours.

"That," says D.U.D.E.A.D., "is how you land a mission."

=== A4-CARDS ===

Saturday night. The maker space again.

Mr. Torres unlocked it just for you. Skylark sits on the workbench where it sat last night, covered in mud. The lid is open. Everyone is crowded around Bitsy's laptop.

There are two tiny memory cards: one from the logger and one from the camera. Everything that happened up there, every reading and every picture, is on those cards, or it isn't.

Bitsy holds up the first card between two fingers. "Ready?"

Nobody is ready. Everyone nods anyway.

=== A4-DARK ===

5:45. The sun touches the edge of the fields and slides down behind them. The sky turns orange, then pink, then gray. The cornstalks turn into black shapes. You can barely see your own sneakers.

Spark has his phone flashlight on. It lights up a circle of dirt about as big as a pizza.

Mr. Torres puts a hand on your shoulder. "That's it," he says gently. "We can't find a box in the dark. We'll just walk past it."

Nobody argues. You all know he's right. You trudge back to the van with mud on your knees and the cold settling in, and nobody says much on the drive home.

Somewhere out there in the dark, Skylark is sitting in the dirt.

=== A4-DARKSEARCH ===

You drive to the middle of the predicted landing ellipse and start walking.

There's no signal to follow. No beeper to listen for. All you have is a pencil oval on a map, and the oval is twelve kilometers long.

You walk fields until your legs ache. Spark climbs a hay bale to look around. Bitsy stops every few minutes, holding her breath, listening for a beep she knows can't come.

You find a fertilizer bag, flapping on a fence. A deflated birthday balloon that says HAPPY 7TH. One work boot, all by itself in a ditch.

You don't find Skylark.

=== A4-GOHOME ===

"Let's go home," you say. "We'll ask for help."

On the drive back, Bitsy writes a post. She attaches the best photo from launch morning: Skylark on the tarp, the balloon swaying above it. *LOST: Small foam science box from a weather balloon, landed somewhere northeast of the county fairgrounds on Saturday. Harmless. If you find it, please call.* She adds Ms. Chen's school number.

She posts it to three local community groups. Spark shares it. Mr. Torres shares it. D.U.D.E.A.D. doesn't have an account, but he reads every comment over Bitsy's shoulder.

People are nice. Somebody says *good luck kids!* Somebody else says *my grandson did one of these!*

Then you wait.
