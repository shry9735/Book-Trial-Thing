Chapter 1 · Opening Scene

Spark's Big
Prototype Problem

A choose-your-own-adventure about electronics

A drone dropped a package on Spark's doorstep at 7:43 in the morning.

It was small. Brown. Covered in stamps. And in the corner, in messy handwriting: From Professor Ohm. Handle with care. Seriously.

Spark grabbed his wire cutters and got to work on the twine.

Inside, packed in foam, was a gadget. It had a small screen, a motor behind a clear plastic window, a tangle of coloured wires, and one big button on the side. There was also a note, crumpled like it had been written in a hurry.

Spark reads aloud "Spark — I need your help. This is my newest prototype. Something's wrong with it and I can't figure out what. The Maker Faire is in THREE DAYS. Please fix it. I believe in you. — Prof. Ohm. P.S. Don't drop it."

Spark stared at the device for exactly two seconds. Then he pressed the button.

The screen flickered on. A loading bar crawled across it. There was a hopeful little ding —

And then everything went wrong at once. The motor stuttered and stopped. A light blinked three times. The screen went blank — and then showed one thing:

?

Spark "Okay. So it's broken. Cool. Cool cool cool."

A whirring sound came from the shelf. Dude-ad — Spark's robot — was waking up. His eyes lit up. His antenna snapped out. His chest screen flashed: READY.

Dude-ad "Morning. Ooh — new hardware. Want me to scan it?"
Spark "Yes. And pull up anything you have on Professor Ohm's builds. Specs, diagrams, whatever you've got."
📋
Dude-ad pulls up a reference Dude-ad projects Professor Ohm's project files — schematics, component lists, power specs. One note near the bottom is highlighted in red: "WARNING: Do not expose to humidity." Nobody mentions this. Yet.

The workshop door banged open. Bitsy skidded in, backpack half-zipped, a piece of toast clamped in her teeth.

Bitsy "Is that an Ohm prototype?! I saw the drone from my window! Can I test it? My workshop has way more outlets than yours."
Spark "It's broken. Three days to fix it. We need to figure out what's wrong first."

All three of them leaned over the workbench. The question mark blinked on the little screen.

Spark "Okay. Let's think. When I pressed the button — what actually happened?"
Bitsy "The screen came on. There was a loading bar."
Spark "Right. And then?"
Bitsy "The motor made that stuttery noise. And the light blinked — three times, I counted."
Spark "And then the screen crashed and showed the question mark. So that's three things. Three separate things."

Spark grabbed a marker and wrote on the whiteboard behind the bench:

Spark — whiteboard 1. Motor stuttered.
2. Light blinked 3×.
3. Screen → ?

He stared at the list. Bitsy stared at the list. Dude-ad's antenna tilted to one side — his thinking pose. Then his chest screen lit up.

💬
Dude-ad responds "Good. Three symptoms. They might all be connected — or they might not be. So: where do you want to start?"

Three problems. Three days. One choice.
