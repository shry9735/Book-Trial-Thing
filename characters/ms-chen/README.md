## Canon

What the books already establish.

**Who she is**
- Their CS and engineering teacher. In Book 2 she helped design the
  original system. *(Book 2 · characters.md; Lost Signal · front.md)*
- Home for the weekend in both books, so she advises by text. *(Book 2 ·
  characters.md; Lost Signal · front.md, structure.md)*
- Shows up in person after: she visits on Monday, pins the photos on the
  wall, apologises to Mrs. Lindqvist alongside you, and her school number
  is the one on Skylark's label. *(Lost Signal · act1.md, endings.md)*

**How she talks**
- Short messages, often a question. Never answers outright. *(Book 2 ·
  characters.md; Lost Signal · structure.md)*
- When she does give direct advice, it's always right. She won't rush
  anyone.
- Never condescending. Uses the engineering word, then says it in plain
  English if it's new.
- Signs off "— Ms. C". *(Book 2; Lost Signal)*

## Proposed

*Everything below is new. None of it is in a book yet. Keep, change or
cut; move what you keep up into Canon.*

### Look

Late thirties, shoulder-length black hair tucked behind one ear,
rectangular dark-framed glasses, a navy cardigan over a striped shirt, a
lanyard of keys, usually holding a coffee mug or her phone.

She has never been drawn, and *Lost Signal* doesn't need her to be. The
look is here for a cover, a classroom scene or the game, if she ever
appears. Status is `proposed` in `cast.yaml`.

### Why she asks instead of tells

Before teaching, Ms. Chen spent eight years as a test engineer at a company
that made medical pumps. Her whole job was asking "what happens if...?"
about other people's designs, and she was very good at it. She left because
she liked the moment someone found the answer themselves more than she
liked finding it.

That's where "test in the conditions where it's going to run" comes from:
in her old job, it was the difference between a lab and a hospital.

**Keep it in the background.** She never tells this story. A reader should
only ever see the questions.

### What she wants, and what she's afraid of

- **Wants** her students to not need her. Every question she texts is a
  question they'll ask themselves next time.
- **Afraid of** the answer that would save them. The hardest part of her
  job is the text she doesn't send. Once per book, let the reader see her
  typing indicator start and stop.

### Habits

- **Signs off "— Ms. C"** when the message is advice, and doesn't when it's
  a question.
- **Answers a question with one question,** never two.
- **Replies fast** on a launch day. Replies at 6 a.m. on a normal day, so
  they know she's been up thinking about it.
- **The checklist.** Every project gets one, and every item on it is a
  decision, not a box to tick.

### People

- **Bitsy:** her most like-minded student, which is why she pushes Bitsy
  hardest on hardware.
- **Spark:** she gives him questions with something physical in them, so he
  has something to hold.
- **Mr. Torres:** her opposite and her ally. She asks; he stops anything
  unsafe. They trust each other completely, and at the start of every
  project they agree on who handles what.

### New lines to try

- *"What would make that wrong?"*
- *"You don't need me for this one. — Ms. C"* The text she sends when she's
  proudest.
- *"What did it actually do? Not what should it do. What did it do?"*
