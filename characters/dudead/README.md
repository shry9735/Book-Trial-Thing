## Canon

What the books, the game and the art already establish.

**Who he is**
- A robot and a friend. "He's not part of the mission, but he's coming
  anyway." *(Lost Signal · front.md)*
- The voice of reason. When Spark and Bitsy argue in circles, he cuts
  through it with what is actually happening. *(Lost Signal · structure.md;
  game · story.js)*
- Talks like a person. No LCD screen, no beeps. *(Lost Signal ·
  structure.md; game · story.js)*
- Has microphones, and that matters: in *Lost Signal*'s Act 4 he's the one
  who hears the beeper. *(Lost Signal · structure.md, act4.md)*
- Leans against the bench with his arms crossed. Stands at the edge of the
  tarp with his hands on his hips, like a coach. Rides up front in the van
  with a paper map, because "paper doesn't need a signal." Raises "the
  part of his visor where an eyebrow would be." *(Lost Signal · act1–3)*
- Doesn't have a social media account, but reads every comment over
  Bitsy's shoulder. *(Lost Signal · act4.md)*
- The classroom mascot: he greets students in the game's classroom with a
  rotating line. *(game · app.py `DUDE_LINES`)*

**How he talks**
- Short. Calls the reader "kid". *(Lost Signal; game)*
- Speaks in rules you could put on a poster: one sentence, then stop.
- Asks the question nobody asked: not "what should we change?" but "what
  is it actually doing?"
- Gives credit plainly: "Measured. Not guessed. Nice."

**Lines he's said**

  *(Lost Signal)*

- *"Now you know turning it off and on again isn't a diagnosis."* *(Lost

- *"The sky is exactly how it's supposed to be. It just doesn't match what

- *"Every part on the list had to earn its spot. That one showed up this

- *"I don't have skin, kid. I have* finish*."* *(Lost Signal)*

**Look**
Kid-sized friendly humanoid robot, rounded white boxy head with a wide
glowing yellow visor showing two big round cartoon eyes and a small smile,
round yellow ear-pieces, thin antenna topped with a yellow ball, white boxy
torso with a chunky gold chain necklace, maroon segmented arms and legs with
gold bolts, oversized yellow sneakers. *(cast.yaml, from Lost Signal's
character sheet; matches the finished art)*

### Two versions of him

**Book 2's manuscript has the original D.U.D.E.A.D.** There he *is* the
project: a line-following robot with a 16×2 LCD, "earnest, a little
anxious", who talks only in short all-caps messages like `REBOOTING...
REBOOTING...`. *(Book 2 · characters.md)*

The game and *Lost Signal* changed him on purpose. He became the friend
who talks, and the game handed the screen, the messages and the breakdowns
to a new robot, [Tracer](../tracer/). Book 2 needs the same change before
it's finished. Tracer's profile has the message bank.

### The "fool" lines

In the game he says "fool" eight times, and the classroom rotates "I pity
the bug!" and "Quit jibber-jabbin'!". With the gold chain, those echo a
living celebrity's famous persona. In the books he never says "fool" and
has a voice of his own, which is both safer and better. See
[Where the sources disagree](../README.md#where-the-sources-disagree), item
4, and the replacement lines below.

## Proposed

*Everything below is new. None of it is in a book or the game yet. Keep,
change or cut; move what you keep up into Canon.*

### Where he came from

The summer before sixth grade, Mr. Torres opened the maker space for a
"build anything" week. Spark and Bitsy, who had never worked together on
purpose, ended up at the same bench because it was the only one left.

Spark built a body out of whatever was in the scrap bins: a white
electrical box for a head, a dented toolbox for a torso, maroon conduit for
arms and legs, and a pair of yellow sneakers someone left in lost and
found. He painted the bolts gold because they were the only paint left.
Bitsy wrote the program.

They never finished him. At the end of the week he could walk about four
steps and say his own name. Then, sometime that autumn, he started saying
other things. Nobody is entirely sure how much of that is Bitsy's code.
Bitsy says "all of it". She doesn't sound sure.

**Leave the last part a mystery.** The stories never need to explain how he
thinks, and kids love an unanswered question.

### What D.U.D.E.A.D. stands for

Book 2's notes ask whether it ever gets revealed. A suggestion:

> **D**ebugging **U**nit, **D**efinitely **E**xperimental, **A**lmost
> **D**one

It's the label Bitsy wrote on his chest in marker on the last day of the
build week, because he wasn't finished. It's still there under the paint.
It makes his name a joke about him, and the joke is the point of him: he's
the one who never pretends something works when it doesn't.

Reveal it late, once, and let a character ask "Almost done with *what*?"

### Where the voice comes from

The maker space has a shelf of old shop-safety videos Mr. Torres keeps "for
historical reasons": a 1980s narrator with a moustache saying things like
*"Measure twice. Cut once. No exceptions."* D.U.D.E.A.D. watched all of
them, during the build week, at double speed, on a loop.

That's why he talks like a coach, in short rules, and why he can't stand a
shortcut. It also gives him a voice that belongs to him, with no echo of
any real person.

### The chain

It's a bike chain. Spark found it in the scrap bin, sprayed it gold to
match the bolts, and hung it round his neck to cover a gap in the
toolbox-torso where the hinge used to be. D.U.D.E.A.D. refuses to take it
off. *"Chains on, brain on"* is about that bike chain.

### What he wants, and what he's afraid of

- **Wants** to see things. He's a robot built out of scrap who has never
  been anywhere, and secretly he's the most amazed person in every room:
  the black sky from thirty kilometres, the first time an LED turns on.
  He covers it with a one-liner. Let the reader catch him at it now and
  then.
- **Afraid of** being finished, in the sense of done with. Every new
  project is a new robot that does one thing well. He does many things
  badly. He's gruff with Tracer and fiercely protective of him, which is
  the same feeling from two sides.

### How he grows

He doesn't change much; he's the fixed point. What changes is how much he
lets show. Each story should have one moment where the brash voice drops
and he says something plainly, then picks the voice straight back up.

### Habits

- **Arms crossed, leaning on something.** The default.
- **Hands on hips** when he's about to make a call.
- **The visor eyebrow.** One side of the visor's light tilts up.
- **Low whistle** when something goes wrong in an interesting way.
- **Says "kid" to everyone,** including Mr. Torres, once. Mr. Torres let it
  go.
- **Counts things.** Packets, beeps, steps. Out loud, if he's nervous.

### People

- **Spark:** built him, and painted him in Spark's own colours. D.U.D.E.A.D.
  is the only one who can tell Spark he's wrong without Spark arguing
  back.
- **Bitsy:** wrote his first program. The only person he calls by name
  instead of "kid", and only when it matters.
- **You:** "kid". He's the reader's coach: never on Spark's side or
  Bitsy's, always on the side of what's true.
- **Tracer:** a little brother he would never admit to. Talks to Tracer
  directly, as if Tracer can hear him. ("Hold it together, little man.")
- **Mr. Torres:** mutual respect between two people who don't say much.

### Replacement lines for the game

To give the game his book voice. Each one keeps the rhythm of the line it
replaces.

| Now | Instead |
|---|---|
| "I pity the bug!" | "Bugs hate me, kid!" |
| "Quit jibber-jabbin'!" | "Less talk, more test!" |
| "Circuits, fool!" | "Circuits, kid!" |
| "No shortcuts, fool!" | "No shortcuts, kid!" |
| "Chains on, brain on!" | Keep, once the chain is a bike chain |

And in *The Day of the Science Fair*: remove ", fool" and the commas
around it, or swap it for "kid", in all eight lines. Each line still works.

### New lines to try

- *"What's it actually doing?"* His question, the one Spark starts saying

- *"Measure twice, kid. No exceptions."* The shop-safety video.
- *"...Huh."* The one time he sees something that leaves him with nothing
