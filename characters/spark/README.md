## Canon

What the books, the game and the art already establish.

**Who he is**
- Built most of the hardware on every project: D.U.D.E.A.D.'s body and
  wiring in Book 2, Tracer in the game, Skylark in *Lost Signal*. "If it's
  something you can touch, Spark understands it." *(Lost Signal · front.md;
  Book 2 · characters.md)*
- Enthusiastic about physical things. Gets excited when he can touch and fix
  something. Genuinely believes hardware is the more interesting problem,
  but isn't dismissive of people about it. *(Book 2 · characters.md)*
- The camera is his favourite part of Skylark. *(Lost Signal · act1.md)*

**How he talks**
- Short sentences. Confident. Direct. *(Book 2 · characters.md)*
- Suggests a physical fix first, whatever the problem is.
- Gets frustrated when it turns out to be the code.

**The rule he lives by**
- Right about hardware: the loose connection, the hardware variation, the
  unreliable breadboard.
- Wrong when he waves off software, or says "just swap it" before anyone
  has looked at what's happening. *(Book 2 · characters.md; Lost Signal ·
  structure.md; game · story.js)*

## Proposed

*Everything below is new. None of it is in a book or the game yet. Keep,
change or cut; move what you keep up into Canon.*

### Where "it's always a wire" comes from

Spark lives with his mom and his grandpa, Abuelo, who ran a TV and radio
repair shop for forty years. The shop is gone, but its contents fill the
garage: shelves of dead radios, a workbench with burn marks, coffee cans of
screws sorted by nobody knows what.

Spark grew up on a stool at that bench. His grandpa's rule was **"follow the
wire"**, and in forty years of radios it was nearly always right: the fault
was a cracked joint, a frayed lead, a connector that had wriggled loose.
*"It's always a wire"* is his grandpa's line, and Spark says it the way you
quote family.

That's also his flaw. The rule was true for radios. Robots have code, and
code doesn't crack or fray. Spark's story across the books is learning
where his grandpa's rule stops.

### Why "Spark"

It's a nickname from his grandpa. He says the boy lights up whenever you
hand him something broken. Nobody at school calls him anything else. His
real name is an [open question](../README.md#open-questions). Leaving it
unrevealed makes a good running gag, and Bitsy is the only one who knows.

### What he wants, and what he's afraid of

- **Wants** to build something that goes where he can't. That's why the
  camera is his favourite part of Skylark: it's his eyes at thirty
  kilometres up. One day he wants to build a rover.
- **Afraid of** being useless. When the problem is in the code, he has
  nothing to hold and nothing to wiggle. He fills that silence by grabbing
  the nearest wire, which is exactly when he's most likely to be wrong.

### How he grows

| Story | Where he is |
|---|---|
| Science fair | "I can feel it." Swaps parts on instinct. Sometimes right, often for the wrong reason ("Just not the one I checked"). |
| *Lost Signal* | Starts to trust a measurement over a feeling. Still waves off the software ideas. |
| Next | Carries a multimeter in his vest as well as a screwdriver, and says "let's measure it" before Bitsy has to. |

The growth is never Spark becoming a programmer. It's learning that "I can
feel it" isn't data.

### Habits

- **The vest has pockets, and the pockets are full:** a small screwdriver,
  zip ties, electrical tape, a spare jumper wire, a pencil stub. When he's
  thinking, he pats them to check they're all there.
- **Calls wires by colour.** "Pull the yellow one." He never says "the
  signal line".
- **Taps things.** Two fingers on a component before he touches it, like
  knocking on a door.
- **Hums when he concentrates,** always the same three notes. Bitsy can tell
  how close he is to fixing something by how fast he hums.
- **Keeps one thing** from every project: a wheel from Tracer, the spare
  sensor from Skylark. They live on a shelf in his room.

### People

- **Bitsy:** best friends since fourth grade, when they were paired for a
  science project and argued for a week about whether a baking-soda volcano
  needed a pump. They argue constantly and never meanly. He'd never say it,
  but he trusts her code more than his own hands.
- **D.U.D.E.A.D.:** Spark built his body and painted him in Spark's own
  colours: maroon, yellow and gold. That's why they match. D.U.D.E.A.D. is
  the only one who can tell Spark he's wrong without Spark arguing back.
- **Mr. Torres:** Spark's favourite grown-up. They both think with their
  hands. Torres won't let him solder alone, and Spark has stopped asking.
- **Abuelo:** comes to the science fair in his good jacket and asks the
  judges about their soldering.

### New lines to try

- *"Follow the wire."* His grandpa's version, said quietly to himself.
- *"Okay. Okay. What's it actually doing?"* Spark, a little older, saying
  D.U.D.E.A.D.'s question before D.U.D.E.A.D. can.
- *"I don't trust anything I can't hold."* To Bitsy, half joking.
- *"That's not a wire."* The first time he says this without being told,
  it's a big moment.
