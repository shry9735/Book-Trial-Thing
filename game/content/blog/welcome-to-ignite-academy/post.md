Ignite Academy teaches electronics and programming to students 13 and up, in a game that runs in the browser. There's nothing to install. A student signs in, walks into the workshop, and starts on the first lesson.

## What students do

Lessons are short and come in three kinds: something to read, something to watch, and something to build or play. They're grouped into tracks (Circuits, Basic Electricity and Code), and within a track they come in order. Nobody meets Ohm's Law before they've met a circuit, and when a lesson isn't open yet, the menu says exactly what unlocks it.

Finish a lesson and there's a short quiz. Get one wrong and you're told why, not just that you missed it. Along the way students collect badges and trinkets in their backpack.

There are eight lessons today, with more on the way.

## What grown-ups see

This is the part we care about most. A parent or teacher doesn't get a score to decode. They get the specific question a student is stuck on, the answer they chose, and the sentence that explains the right one. We wrote more about that in [The wrong answer is the useful part](/blog/the-wrong-answer-is-the-useful-part).

Parents can also pick a grade and see how their child's work lines up with the science, computer science and maths standards most US states build on.

## Getting started

- **Students** join with the class code from their teacher.
- **Parents** sign up with their child's class code and link their child using the student code on the child's page.
- **Teachers**, including parents teaching at home, can start a class and get a join code to hand out.

You can create an account from the top of any page.
