Most learning tools can tell you a student scored 39%. Very few can tell you what to do about it.

A percentage says something went wrong. It doesn't say what. A parent reading it has no idea where to start, and a teacher reading thirty of them still has to guess who needs help first.

## What Ignite shows instead

When a student gets a quiz question wrong and hasn't fixed it yet, their progress page lists:

- **the question** they're stuck on,
- **the answer they chose**,
- **the correct answer**, and
- **one sentence** explaining why it's right.

So instead of "39%", a parent reads something like: *What's a resistor's actual job in a circuit? They chose "it stores charge for later." Correct: it limits the current flowing through it.* That's something you can talk through at the kitchen table in a couple of minutes.

## The class sorts itself

Teachers get the same detail for every student, plus a short list at the top of their dashboard called *Worth a look*: the student who's stuck, the one who hasn't practised in eleven days, the ones who haven't started yet. Everyone else is doing fine, and the page says so instead of making you check.

There are no red marks and no rankings. Just plain sentences a person can act on.

## Help for the grown-ups

Some lessons come with material just for parents and teachers: a short explainer for a grown-up who last did this in school, or an answer key with the reasoning written out. Students can't open them.
