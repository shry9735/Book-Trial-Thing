# Helping with Basic Electricity

You do not need to know any of this yourself. The most useful thing you can
do is ask questions you genuinely don't know the answer to — that is what
makes a child explain, and explaining is where the learning happens.

## What this track is actually about

Three quantities and how they relate:

| | What it is | A rough analogy |
|---|---|---|
| **Voltage** (volts, V) | How hard the electricity is being pushed | Water pressure in a pipe |
| **Current** (amps, A) | How much is flowing | How much water goes past per second |
| **Resistance** (ohms, Ω) | How much the path fights back | How narrow the pipe is |

Ohm's Law ties them together: **V = I × R**. Push harder and more flows.
Narrow the pipe and less flows.

The water analogy is useful and also wrong in places. If your child says
"but water doesn't…" — that is a good sign. Analogies are scaffolding, and
noticing where one breaks is real understanding.

## The maths it leans on

This is the part that trips students up, and it is not the electronics:

- Multiplying and dividing whole numbers
- Rearranging a formula — if V = I × R, then I = V ÷ R and R = V ÷ I

If your child can do the first but not the second, that is almost certainly
where they are stuck. It is a maths gap wearing an electronics costume, and
it is worth ten minutes on its own.

## Four questions worth asking

1. "What's the voltage, and what's the current?" — checks they can read the
   circuit at all before doing any arithmetic.
2. "If I doubled the resistance, what happens to the current?" — checks they
   understand the relationship rather than having memorised one arrangement.
3. "Show me which way you rearranged it." — this is where the mistake almost
   always is.
4. "Does that answer look sensible?" — a current of 4,000 amps from a AA
   battery should feel wrong. Estimating is a skill of its own.

## When they are stuck

Resist telling them the answer. "What do you already know?" gets further
than a hint, and much further than a correction. If they are stuck for more
than about ten minutes, the problem is usually one step back from where
they are looking.

## Safety

Everything in this track is battery-powered and low voltage. Nothing here
should ever be plugged into a wall socket. If your child asks about mains
electricity — good question, right answer is "never, that one can kill you."
