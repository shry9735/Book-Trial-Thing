# Your first circuit, at home

## What you need

Nothing, to start with. The lessons work on their own, and a kit only helps
once your child wants to build the thing rather than read about it.

If you do get a kit: a breadboard, some jumper wires, a few LEDs, a handful
of resistors and a battery pack covers this whole track. Nothing in it is
dangerous and nothing plugs into a wall.

## The idea that has to land

A breadboard's holes are connected underneath in a pattern that is not
obvious from looking at it. Rows are joined; the long rails down the sides
are joined separately. Almost every "it doesn't work" in this track is a
component in the right-looking hole that is not the right hole.

If your child is stuck on a circuit that should work, ask them to trace the
path with a finger, out loud, from the battery's positive terminal all the
way back to the negative one. Nine times in ten they find it themselves.

## Why nothing works the first time

Because that is normal, and it is worth saying so. Breadboard connections
are held by friction. Wires come loose. LEDs only work one way round. A
resistor in the wrong place stops the whole thing.

Professionals build circuits that do not work the first time too. The
difference is they expect it and check methodically instead of getting
discouraged.

## An LED only works one way round

The longer leg is positive. Backwards, it simply does nothing — it is not
broken and nothing is damaged. This catches everybody once.

## Questions to ask

- "Trace the circuit for me — where does the electricity go?"
- "Which leg of the LED is longer?"
- "What is the resistor doing there?" (Protecting the LED. Without it the
  LED gets too much current and dies, usually quite dramatically.)
