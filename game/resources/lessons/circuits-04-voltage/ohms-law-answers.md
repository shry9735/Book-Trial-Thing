# Voltage & Ohm's Law — answers and worked reasoning

**Answer key — for grown-ups only.**

## Quiz

The point of each question, and what a wrong answer usually means.

**"What does the V in V = I × R stand for?"** — Voltage. A wrong answer
here means the symbols have not been connected to the words yet, and no
amount of practice with the formula will help until they are. Go back to
naming the three quantities out loud.

**Rearranging for current.** I = V ÷ R. The most common wrong answer is
I = V × R, which is the formula copied rather than rearranged. Ask them to
check with numbers they know: 9 volts across 3 ohms cannot be 27 amps.

**Rearranging for resistance.** R = V ÷ I. Students who got the previous
one right almost always get this one right too; a student who gets one and
not the other has memorised rather than understood.

## A worked example to talk through

> A 9 V battery drives 0.003 A through a resistor. What is its resistance?

R = V ÷ I = 9 ÷ 0.003 = **3,000 Ω** (3 kΩ).

Two things worth pausing on, and they are the whole lesson:

- **The decimal.** Dividing by 0.003 rather than 3 is where most errors
  live. Ask: "is the answer going to be bigger or smaller than 9?" Dividing
  by something less than 1 makes it bigger. Many students are surprised.
- **The units.** 3,000 Ω is 3 kΩ. Both are right; engineers say the second.

## If they are stuck

The three most common causes, in the order to check them:

1. **They cannot rearrange the formula.** A maths gap, not an electronics
   one. Practise with any formula at all — area = length × width works.
2. **They lose track of the decimal point.** Have them estimate first, then
   calculate, then compare.
3. **They are reading the circuit wrong** — using the wrong voltage, or
   adding resistances that are in parallel. Ask them to point at the parts
   of the diagram as they name the numbers.
